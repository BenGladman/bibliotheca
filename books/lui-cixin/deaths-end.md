We get a woman protagonist again in this installment, but Lui puts her through the ringer. At first she is portrayed as highly intelligent, but as the story progresses she appears more as an ingénue, with little agency, things just happen to her. Somehow she ends up making several huge, globally important decisions, but all the decisions she makes end up being disastrous. I felt there was some misogyny going on here.

Lui is fond of describing idyllic future worlds for a few pages and then having them destroyed in disaster. It’s a trick he uses a couple of times too often. But there are a good few interesting ideas throughout which kept me reading.
